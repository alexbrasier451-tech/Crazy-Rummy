# Stage 1.1.1 three-way concept decision

**Decision state:** **Owner-approved — Concept B, Compartment Table**  
**Approval date:** 30 July 2026  
**Approval record:** Project-owner statement in the controlling Codex task  
**Common direction:** Midnight Limited  
**Approval fixtures:** `/lobby` healthy/empty-offline and `/game` busy six-seat
player-scoped state

The three concepts share only product truth, the approved visual north star,
and the same deterministic fixture. Their composition, depth, card staging,
information hierarchy, and environmental idea are intentionally different.

## Concept A — Night Timetable

**Lobby composition.** A paired create/join launch ticket sits above an
irregular ruled departure ledger. Open tables are full-width destination slips,
not equal dashboard cards. A narrow signal rail owns freshness and connection
truth.

**Gameplay composition.** An asymmetric baize worktable puts public meld
pockets left/centre and a tall stock/discard decision tower right. Six players
occupy a compact status rail around it; the private hand lives in a low,
shallow foreground tray.

**Depth and environment.** Near-black page depth, enamel route board, recessed
baize, paper slips, then bright cards. One short active-turn route is allowed.

**Crazy Rummy specificity.** The departure ledger maps naturally to open-table
discovery, thirteen hands, current wild, communal melds, and turn progression.

**Interaction and accessibility protection.** Status is always text plus
shape; cards retain explicit labels and full targets; the ledger remains a
semantic list; public, private, and action regions retain their logical order.

**Disposition.** Retain its departure-list readability. Do not select it as
the primary direction: even at high craft it remains close to a premium
scoreboard/dashboard and underplays the six-seat table relationship.

## Concept B — Compartment Table

**Lobby composition.** A narrow illuminated threshold board leads to paired
ticket-gate actions. Available tables emerge as offset perforated invitations
from a dark corridor; one invitation is dominant, not one item in an equal
grid. The offline/empty state becomes an unpunched ticket resting on the rail.

**Gameplay composition.** Six labelled seat plaques form an adaptive perimeter
around a deep baize table lens. Stock/discard occupy the central spine, public
melds dock into labelled sidings, the local hand becomes a curved foreground
tray, and the current decision occupies a full-width conductor call below it.

**Depth and environment.** Dark edge void, enamel fascia, recessed baize,
raised public cards, foreground private cards, and a restrained reflection in
inactive edge space.

**Crazy Rummy specificity.** Two-to-six seating, communal melds, a private
local hand, the current wild, and the thirteen-hand route become one spatial
system. It reads immediately as Crazy Rummy without literal train scenery,
casino decoration, or a generic panel stack.

**Interaction and accessibility protection.**

- DOM and reading order remain header → connection/turn → shared table →
  private hand → actions, regardless of visual placement.
- Opponents expose names, markers, card counts, turn/connection text, and
  scores, never private card identities.
- Interactive cards retain 44 × 44 CSS-pixel targets, redundant labels, and
  non-drag operation.
- The compact phone uses an adaptive partial perimeter and an internally
  scrolling public table rather than shrinking a desktop oval.
- Reduced motion removes travel, flip, fan spring, reflection, parallax, and
  route drawing.
- Forced colours removes material imagery and uses system colours, 2 px
  boundaries, text labels, checks, and border patterns.

**Disposition.** **Owner-approved direction.** It is the largest structural
departure from the beta's panel/list presentation while remaining truthful,
legible, responsive, and unmistakably a card game.

## Concept C — Route Atlas

**Lobby composition.** Open tables become stops on one bold thirteen-station
abstract route. Create and Join are detachable terminal stubs; occupancy is
shown through ticket punches and station markers.

**Gameplay composition.** Public melds become labelled carriages on a
horizontal route spine. Stock/discard is the current junction, scores form a
narrow ticket ledger, and the local hand enters as a long foreground strip.

**Depth and environment.** Mostly flat editorial print and embossing against a
deep black field, rather than a physical tabletop.

**Crazy Rummy specificity.** It makes the changing wild schedule and
thirteen-hand match unusually legible, and provides a strong language for
results and route progress.

**Interaction and accessibility protection.** Every stop and state has a text
label; the route is supplementary rather than the only progress cue; the card
strip scrolls without sub-target overlap; forced colours reduces the map to
ordered, outlined stations.

**Disposition.** Retain its thirteen-stop notation for the selected
direction's header and results language. Reject it as the primary gameplay
composition because the route competes with the immediate draw/discard choice
and can turn readable melds into a diagram.
